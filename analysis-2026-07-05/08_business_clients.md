# 08 — Business / Clients

**Applicability check:** no client work, no revenue, no invoices, no pricing artifacts exist in this repo. But the report is *not* skipped, because revenue intent is documented: the plan's first sentence is "You want to start a business around a co-parenting/shared-custody coordination tool" (plan line 4), and session memory frames both FTP and SparkPlay as businesses. So this is a pre-revenue business audit — short, because pre-revenue means most sections are honestly empty.

## Pricing discipline

None exists yet, and none is overdue — the product doesn't exist. What IS overdue for a self-described business: **any evidence of the commercial side at all.** After 24 days there is no landing-page copy beyond a heading ("src/app/page.tsx" is a minimal landing), no domain purchase evidence, no waitlist, no competitor pricing notes in the repo, no target price point anywhere in the 120-line FTP plan. The plan is 100% build and 0% sell. For contrast, the market it enters is established and priced (OurFamilyWizard ~US$100+/yr/parent, TalkingParents, 2houses — none researched anywhere in the visible planning). Building 11 milestones before one competitive-pricing note is a sequencing risk the owner should at least consciously accept.

## Undeployed assets

The whole ledger from 02: ~AUD $3,000–6,500 of discounted build value, all of it undeployed. Additionally undeployed per memory: the ADHDan merch store (built, blocked on a manual setup punch list — actual sellable inventory sitting behind ~hours of account setup) — that one is *closer* to first-dollar than FTP is by roughly ten milestones. If the goal this quarter is "any revenue at all," the evidence says finish the merch punch list, not FTP M4.

## The charge-vs-worth gap

Not applicable in the client sense (no clients). The solo-founder version of the gap: the owner's build labor is being invested at $0 realized against a market that (per 02) prices this work at $80–150/h. That's fine — every founder does it — but it's only rational if concentrated toward *something that ships*. Split across six projects with zero deploys, the same hours realize $0 across all of them. Concentration is the pricing discipline available at this stage.

## What's working (receipts)
- The two business ideas both have genuine, researched differentiation on file: FTP's swap-request-with-atomic-override model (plan lines 51–53), SparkPlay's materials-on-hand generation gap validated against five competitors (plan line 128). These are real theses, not vibes.

## What's not (receipts)
- Zero commercial artifacts for a 24-day-old "business" (this repo, everywhere).
- Business #2 (SparkPlay) was started before business #1 shipped anything; business #1 was never formally deprioritized (03 §aging-7, 04).

## Improvements (ranked by leverage)
1. **Choose the business** (04's recommendation stands: evidence favors SparkPlay) and give the other an explicit parked status · effort **S**.
2. **Spend one hour on competitor pricing for whichever is chosen** and write the target price into its plan — it will shape MVP scope more cheaply now than after 8 more milestones · effort **S**.
3. **Bank the nearest dollar:** ADHDan merch punch list to completion, purely to break the portfolio-wide $0-revenue seal and learn the ship-motion · effort **M**.

## STOP doing
Stop calling things businesses in planning documents while giving them zero commercial artifacts — either add the minimum (price point, competitor note, a way to reach one real user) or file them honestly as learning projects. The label matters because it silently justifies unbounded build time.

## Top-3 for this area
1. One chosen business, in writing.
2. One pricing/competitor note in its plan.
3. One dollar of revenue anywhere in the portfolio.
